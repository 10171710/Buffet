Our story

Hearth & Harvest began with forty seats, one oven and a simple idea: a meal tastes better when everyone at the table gets to choose their own plate.

How it started

A small kitchen with big appetites

In 2009 Samir Haddad’s parents opened a forty-seat dining room on Orchard Lane, cooking the dishes they grew up with and the ones their neighbours kept asking for.

Marco Bellini joined the pass a year later, and the menu started to travel: a tandoor from Lucknow, fresh pasta from Emilia, a wok that never seems to cool down. Customers could not agree on a single favourite, so in 2013 we set out the first weekend buffet and let everyone build their own.

Seventeen years on we are still family-run. The dining room is bigger, the stations are live and the menu changes every week — but the first rule has not moved: nobody leaves hungry, and nothing good goes to waste.

Guiding our work

Gather round, and make room for everyone

We make fresh, generous food for every family in Maplewood. Four commitments guide us at each meal, all year long.

Welcome every diner

Step-free access, high chairs and fresh menus help all guests feel welcome. Everyone deserves a comfortable place at our table.

Grown right near home

Seasonal produce, eggs and dairy arrive from nearby growers. We cook in small batches to keep each dish fresh each day.

Waste less, feed more

Careful portions keep buffets fresh; we chill surplus and share it through our community kitchen, to nourish neighbours.

Serve guests with warmth

We remember guest names, offer thoughtful help and make every visit feel warm, relaxed and easy to enjoy together.

Our history

Seventeen years, one family table

From forty seats to a 180-seat restaurant with a garden patio — the milestones that shaped Hearth & Harvest.

  1. 2009

    Opened with 40 seats

    A single dining room, a handful of family recipes and a hand-painted sign on Orchard Lane.

  2. 2013

    First weekend buffet

    Regulars could never choose, so we let them try everything. The Saturday buffet sold out in its first week.

  3. 2016

    Family hall added

    Sixty seats with high chairs, a kids’ corner and colouring kits — built after listening to the parents who kept asking.

  4. 2020

    Community kitchen opens

    When neighbours needed support, we started cooking for them. The community kitchen now feeds 300 neighbours a week.

  5. 2024

    Renovation to 180 seats

    A new dining room, six live stations and a 40-seat garden patio, with step-free access to every table.

  6. 2026

    Our 17th year

    Rated 4.8 stars from more than 2,900 reviews — and still run by the same family, cooking for the same neighbours.

  • 180seats today, up from 40
  • 80+dishes, rotating weekly
  • 300neighbours fed each week
